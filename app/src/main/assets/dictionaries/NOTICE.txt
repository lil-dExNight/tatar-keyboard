Dictionary data notice

Both shipped dictionaries are derived from downloadable text corpora in the
Leipzig Corpora Collection.

tatar_top100k_v1.tdict.zlib

Derived from the Tatar corpora tat_mixed_2015_1M, tat_news_2015_1M, and
tat_web_2018_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/tat

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the three word-frequency lists were
decoded, validated, normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the modern Tatar
Cyrillic alphabet and a 64-code-point limit, merged with summed frequencies,
ranked, cut to 100,000 forms, lexically reordered, packed into a versioned
binary format with SHA-256, and zlib-compressed. The asset additionally
carries inflected and derived word forms generated by the project's own
build-time paradigm generator (scripts/wordform_gen.py) and admitted only when
attested in tat_mixed_2015_1M, in tat_web_2018_1M or in the committed
conversational frequency table data/dictionary/dict-accept/conv-freq-tt.tsv
(derived from the Tatoeba and OpenSubtitles collections described below) --
tat_news_2015_1M deliberately takes no part in the admission -- with the
frequency set to the corpus count; the cap is 110,000 entries.

The ranking of the Tatar entries additionally carries per-word bonus counts
from three further collections (only counts enter the asset, never text):
the MADLAD-400 Tatar clean split, the tt.wikipedia pages, and the HPLT 2.0
tat_Cyrl documents. Each source was counted with the same normalization,
filtered per word against Russian bleed, and merged with a per-source weight;
the weights and the merge rule are documented in
data/dictionary/dict-accept/bonus-freq-tt.tsv.

tatar_sentstart_v1.txt

Derived from the Tatar corpora tat_mixed_2015_1M and tat_web_2018_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/tat

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the sentence-initial token of every
sentence of the two corpora was counted with the same normalization as the
dictionary above, the counts were filtered to the words the shipped Tatar
dictionary carries, and the top 64 were written as a plain word/frequency
table (scripts/sentstart_pack.py).

Conversational word frequencies -- PACKED since 1.9.0, WIDENED in 1.9.1

Two further collections were measured, turned into word-by-word acceptance
queues (data/dictionary/ru-conv-review.tsv, data/dictionary/tt-conv-review.tsv)
and accepted and packed into the two .tdict.zlib files above.

1.9.0 accepted a word only if a second independent source confirmed it and the
capitalization evidence did not mark it a proper noun. 1.9.1 widened the rule
after a manual review of a hundred randomly chosen words it had turned away
found them to be ordinary language: it accepts the whole queue except formal
fragments -- words shorter than four letters or with no vowel at all, which in
these corpora are subtitle OCR debris -- and one word named individually. The
Tatar half uses only the no-vowel half of that test, because Tatar words are
shorter and a length cut removes living ones. The rule is implemented by
scripts/dict_accept.py; the accepted and rejected words with their counts are
in data/dictionary/dict-accept/.

32 833 Russian forms and 1 993 Tatar forms are inside the assets and carry their
conversational counts. The Russian dictionary's other 67 167 entries, and every
frequency in them, are still Leipzig's; the Tatar dictionary's remaining
110 000 - 1 993 entries split into 88 962 Leipzig forms and a further 19 045
generated word forms, admitted as the tatar_top100k_v1 section above describes
and carrying their corpus counts.

OpenSubtitles, taken from the OPUS collection (OpenSubtitles v2024)
http://www.opensubtitles.org/
Recommended citation: P. Lison, J. Tiedemann, "OpenSubtitles2016: Extracting
Large Parallel Corpora from Movie and TV Subtitles", LREC 2016.
OpenSubtitles publishes NO license grant for this text -- not an unclear
license, an absent one. What the collection does ask is a link back to
opensubtitles.org from anything built on its data; that link is on the line
above and on the app's "Data sources" screen. The project uses the data
anyway, with the absence of a grant stated here as an accepted risk rather
than a settled question; docs/HISTORY.md lists the archived record of the
decision and the measurements behind it.

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: lines are deduplicated, tokens are
normalized to NFC lowercase and filtered to the language alphabet, proper nouns
are removed by capitalization evidence, forms seen fewer than three times are
dropped, and only counts are kept -- with one stated exception. The acceptance
queues can also be cut into review portions by scripts/review_batches.py, and a
word that cannot be judged by its spelling alone carries one short example
sentence there so a reader can judge it. Those sentences come from Tatoeba
ONLY, which is what CC BY 2.0 FR grants in exchange for the citation above,
and they appear only in the review portions, not in the app. No OpenSubtitles
sentence is reproduced anywhere in this repository; words seen only there carry
a list of their neighbouring words with counts instead, which is a fact about
the text and not the text.
No sentence from either collection is stored in the app.

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: documents were language-filtered
(HPLT's own per-document and per-segment labels), split into tokens,
normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the Tatar alphabet, and counted per

dropped. Only the resulting per-word counts, merged with the other sources by
the documented weights, enter the asset; no text from these collections is
stored in the app.
