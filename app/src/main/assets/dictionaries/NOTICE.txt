Dictionary data notice

Both shipped dictionaries are derived from downloadable text corpora in the Leipzig
Corpora Collection.

tatar_top100k_v1.tdict.zlib

Derived from the Tatar corpora tat_mixed_2015_1M, tat_news_2015_1M, and
tat_web_2018_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/tat

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the three word-frequency lists were
decoded, validated, normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the modern Tatar
Cyrillic alphabet and a 64-code-point limit, merged with summed frequencies,
ranked, cut to 100,000 forms, lexically reordered, packed into a versioned
binary format with SHA-256, and zlib-compressed. Since 2026-09-20 the asset
additionally carries inflected and derived word forms generated by the
project's own build-time paradigm generator and admitted only when attested in
the corpora above (frequency = corpus count); the cap is 110,000 entries
(docs/TT-SUGGESTIONS.md).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
tatar_sentstart_v1.txt

Derived from the Tatar corpora tat_mixed_2015_1M and tat_web_2018_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/tat

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the sentence-initial token of every
sentence of the two corpora was counted with the same normalization as the
dictionary above, the counts were filtered to the words the shipped Tatar
dictionary carries, and the top 64 were written as a plain word/frequency
table (docs/TT-SUGGESTIONS.md, phase P4).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
russian_top100k_v1.tdict.zlib

Derived from the Russian corpora rus_news_2022_1M, rus_news_2019_1M, and
rus_wikipedia_2021_1M.
Catalogue page: https://wortschatz.uni-leipzig.de/en/download/Russian

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: the three word-frequency lists were
decoded, validated, normalized to NFC lowercase, filtered to the 33-letter
Russian alphabet (with "e" and "yo" kept apart, and hyphenated and Latin forms
excluded) and a 64-code-point limit, merged with summed frequencies, ranked, cut
to 100,000 forms, lexically reordered, packed into the same versioned binary
format with SHA-256, and zlib-compressed.

Conversational word frequencies -- PACKED since 1.9.0, WIDENED in 1.9.1

Two further collections were measured, turned into word-by-word acceptance queues
(docs/DICTIONARY-RU-CONV-REVIEW.tsv, docs/DICTIONARY-TT-CONV-REVIEW.tsv) and, on
2026-08-24, accepted and packed into the two .tdict.zlib files above.

1.9.0 accepted a word only if a second independent source confirmed it and the
capitalization evidence did not mark it a proper noun; docs/DICT-ACCEPT.md states that
rule and its counts. The operator then read a hundred random words it had turned away,
judged them ordinary language, and lifted the bar: 1.9.1 accepts the whole queue except
formal fragments -- words shorter than four letters or with no vowel at all, which in
these corpora are subtitle OCR debris -- and one word named individually. The Tatar half
uses only the no-vowel half of that test, because Tatar words are shorter and a length
cut removes living ones. docs/DICT-WIDEN.md states the widened rule, every count, and
what it still leaves out.

32 833 Russian forms and 303 Tatar forms are inside the assets and carry their
conversational counts; the other 67 167 and 99 697 entries, and every frequency in them,
are still Leipzig's.

OpenSubtitles, taken from the OPUS collection (OpenSubtitles v2024)
http://www.opensubtitles.org/
Recommended citation: P. Lison, J. Tiedemann, "OpenSubtitles2016: Extracting Large
Parallel Corpora from Movie and TV Subtitles", LREC 2016.
OpenSubtitles publishes NO license grant for this text -- not an unclear license, an
absent one. What the collection does ask is a link back to opensubtitles.org from
anything built on its data; that link is on the line above and on the app's "Data
sources" screen. The project's operator decided on 2026-08-24 to use the data
anyway, with the absence of a grant stated. docs/CORPUS-OS.md records the decision
and the measurements; docs/PUBLISH-CHECKLIST.md carries it to release as a known
risk rather than a settled question.

Changes by the Tatar Keyboard project: lines are deduplicated, tokens are normalized
to NFC lowercase and filtered to the language alphabet, proper nouns are removed by
capitalization evidence, forms seen fewer than three times are dropped, and only
counts are kept -- with one stated exception. The acceptance queues are also readable
in portions (docs/review-batches/), and a word that cannot be judged by its spelling
alone carries one short example sentence there so a reader can judge it. Those
sentences come from Tatoeba ONLY, which is what CC BY 2.0 FR grants in exchange for
the citation above, and they live in the repository, not in the app. No OpenSubtitles
sentence is reproduced anywhere in this repository; words seen only there carry a list
of their neighbouring words with counts instead, which is a fact about the text and
not the text.
No sentence from either collection is stored in the app.
